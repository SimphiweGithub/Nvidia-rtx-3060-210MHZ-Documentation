# Reported repairs for matching GPU telemetry symptoms — 7 November 2026

## Why this changes the investigation

A repairer's public descriptions report component-level repairs for machines showing the distinctive combination of approximately 752 W GPU power and a 210 MHz clock. These are self-reported commercial repair cases, not independently verified diagnoses, and the video footage has not been reviewed. They nevertheless make a board-level power-monitor circuit a concrete diagnostic avenue rather than an inference from the numbers alone.

- A [Dell G15 5511 case](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1FH16B8EgT/) reports 752 W and 210 MHz, then claims that replacement of a power-monitor chip restored clocks above 1700 MHz and approximately 130 W under load. The description does not identify the chip, the GPU, driver version, test duration, or board revision.
- A [Mechrevo case](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1xMi2BcEHK) reports the same 752.7 W and 210 MHz symptoms, corrosion in the power-monitor circuit, and normal power and performance after cleaning and component replacement. The board and chip are not identified in the description.
- An [independent repair shop's Dell G15 video](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1BJAfzsERQ/) claims a clock-lock issue was resolved by replacing a power chip, but its description does not state the power reading or model variant. It is weaker evidence of an exact match.

Another [MSI repair description](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1E9ksBrEP9/) names NCP45495, but concerns a different machine with fluctuating readings, not evidence that this computer has that part. No part number should be ordered or replaced based on these reports. The user's installed GPU is a Dell-subsystem RTX 3060 laptop GPU, while its reported VBIOS version matches a saved Mechrevo-labeled image; neither fact proves board identity or that these repair cases use the same circuit.

For context, [onsemi's public NCP45495 datasheet](https://www.onsemi.com/download/data-sheet/pdf/ncp45495-d.pdf) describes a multi-channel voltage and current monitor with an analog output and control interface. This illustrates why an external monitor or its surrounding sense circuit is worth investigating and why merely altering an NVML reading would not necessarily correct the control input. It does not establish that the part is fitted to this board or that its interface is the local failure. A web-hosted schematic search result mixed LA-K452P and LA-K661P identifiers and displayed confidentiality markings, so its extracted part and net data were not used to identify this motherboard or prescribe repair.

The public datasheet describes the hypothetical part's output as multiplexed analog measurements intended for an external ADC; its recommended normal multiplexer-select period is 0.185–11 microseconds (table 5). If this specific device and mode were eventually verified on the board, a safe physical emulator would have to follow that timing and preserve all monitored channels and protection behavior; a slowly updated fixed wattage or ordinary user-mode polling would not establish that. The datasheet does not specify a 752.67 W failure value, and its search for saturation or open-circuit behavior provides no basis to tie the observed constant to this part. No live probing or spoofing was done.

## Practical implications

Windows reports `Dell G15 5510` and Dell baseboard product `0H0F1D`, revision `A00`. A [motherboard listing](https://www.ebay.com/itm/406308613582) associates this product number with Compal `GDL55 LA-K661P`; this is a seller's identification, not a direct reading of the marking on the user's board. A [schematic index](https://chambersdesigninc.com/file/compal-la-k661p-rev-1-0-pdf/) lists a rev 1.0 schematic for that board. Confirm the printed board number and revision before relying on a schematic. Neither source establishes the actual power-monitor chip or its location, and no schematic was examined here.

The next hardware-specific step is a qualified board-level diagnosis of the actual motherboard and GPU power-monitor path, including inspection for corrosion or damaged components, identification of the monitor IC from the board/schematic, its supply and communication, and the associated current-sense circuit. Measurements and any repair must preserve functional power and thermal protection. The recorded 752.67 W, 210 MHz, active software power cap, independent whole-system battery discharge around 55–62 W, Linux reproduction, and modern-driver behavior are useful symptoms to provide a technician. A component fault is still unproven here; a software/firmware cause remains possible.

No hardware work or changes were performed in this investigation. No software spoof value can be derived from these reports, and no normal-boost or long-duration-protection result has been established on this machine.
